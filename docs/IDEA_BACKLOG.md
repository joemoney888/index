## Idea 5 - London-to-New-York session reversal bias (NAS100, daily-level, cheap and well powered)
- Source: the same presenter as the idea-1 video, in a later video (sells a VIP room and a trading robot; claims a "79.45% win rate over 1,000 back-tests", which is a win rate, not an expectancy). Sanity check: a 79% win rate with the 1.5R targets he shows would mean about +0.97R per trade (0.79 x 1.5 - 0.21), which no real strategy delivers; so the claim implies break-even/partial exits or cherry-picking. Ignore it. The testable piece is his "step two": "if London is bearish, look for New York to be bullish" (the NY session reverses the London push).
- Hypothesis (one, signed): the sign of the London-session return predicts the OPPOSITE sign for the New York session return on NAS100. Related literature (intraday momentum) suggests the opposite sign, so the prior is low; the test is two-sided in spirit but pre-registered as the claimed direction.
- DRAFT rule (to be frozen after review): London return = open 08:00 to close 13:30 UTC (ONE definition; reviewer to advise on DST). NY return = open at 14:30 UTC to the 21:00 UTC cash close, adjusted for DST by using New York clock times (09:30 to 16:00). Signal = sign of London return, traded opposite at the 09:30 New York open, flat at 16:00 New York. Skip days where |London return| < 0.1% of price (noise filter, set ONCE). No stop (or a fixed 1.5 x daily ATR stop; reviewer to advise ONE). N +1.
- Data: NAS100 5-minute bars Nov 2021 to Nov 2026 (about 1,300 trading days; far more power than the 1-minute ideas). Costs: per-bar spread, gap-aware fills, no financing (intraday).
- Power (estimate): about 1,000 trades after the noise filter; per-trade SD about 0.9% of price; detectable mean about 2.64 x 0.9% / sqrt(1000) = about 0.075% of price per trade (about 0.1R if 1R is about 0.7% of price). Much better powered than ideas 1-4, but still only detects a moderate effect.
- Prior: about 5% (the claimed direction), because published intraday-momentum results point the other way.
- Kill rule: validation mean < +0.03R or day-clustered t < 2.0 closes it. No re-tuning of the session boundaries or the noise filter afterwards. Because the sign is pre-registered, a NEGATIVE significant result would be recorded as "opposite sign found", a separate, registered hypothesis (N +1) and NOT traded or tuned in place.
- Questions for the reviewer: (1) is the session definition unambiguous across daylight-saving changes? (2) is the noise filter an extra look to drop? (3) should the opposite-sign (momentum) hypothesis be registered now, in the same family (N +2), so we do not "discover" it later? (4) is a stop needed or does it add a degree of freedom?

## Idea 6 - Intraday momentum: the first part of the day predicts the last half-hour (NAS100)  [Claude's own suggestion, from published research, not from a video]
- Source: Gao, Han, Li and Zhou, "Market intraday momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 129(2), 2018, pp. 394-414 (SPY and other ETFs; the return from the previous close to 10:00 New York, the "first half-hour", predicts the return of the LAST half-hour of the day). Peer-reviewed, with out-of-sample/replication work by others; effects after publication may have shrunk, so the prior is modest. This is the OPPOSITE sign of idea 5 (reversal) and is consistent with our own B.1 finding that fading the BFG entries was worse than random placement.
- Mechanism claimed: hedging/rebalancing demand late in the day pushes price in the direction of the day's early move; informed traders and leveraged-ETF rebalancing add to it.
- DRAFT rule (to be frozen after review): signal = sign of the return from the previous 16:00 New York close to the 10:00 New York close (ONE definition; weekend/holiday handled by using the previous trading session). Trade = long if positive, short if negative, entered at the 15:30 New York bar open, exited at the 16:00 New York price (ONE horizon). No stop. One trade per day. Skip days with a half-day close. N +1 (a second registered predictor, the 15:00-15:30 return, may be added as N +1 if the reviewer wants it, decided BEFORE running).
- Data: NAS100 5-minute bars, Nov 2021 to Nov 2026 (about 1,300 trading days). Costs: both spread crossings from the bar data (median about 0.4 bp each, so about 0.8 bp round trip, to be checked from the data) plus nothing else (30-minute hold, no financing). Metric: net return per trade in basis points (no R: there is no stop), plus the sign consistency across calendar years.
- Power (estimate): last-half-hour return SD about 0.2-0.3% of price per day (to be measured); SE of the mean over about 1,300 days is about 0.007%; detectable at t of 2.64 is about 0.018%, i.e. about 1.8 bp per trade. The cost is about 0.8 bp. So an effect of 2-3 bp net per trade is detectable; smaller than that is not. Far better powered than ideas 1-4.
- Prior: about 10-15% that a positive net effect survives costs in this window.
- Kill rule: net mean per trade <= 0 bp on validation, or day-clustered t < 2.0, or the sign differs between the validation year and the exam year, closes it. No re-tuning of the window boundaries, the horizon or the sign convention afterwards. A significant NEGATIVE effect (reversal) is recorded as a separate hypothesis (N +1), not traded in place.
- Questions for the reviewer: (1) is the first-period definition (previous close to 10:00) the right replication, given that NAS100 CFD trades almost 24 hours and the "previous close" is the 16:00 New York cash close? (2) is it a problem that the cash-index close is not tradable on the CFD at exactly 16:00? (3) should the exam be the last 365 days, as in the rest of the project? (4) should the second predictor be registered now?

## Idea 7 - Confidence-scored position sizing with fractional Kelly (Joe's idea, 23 Nov 2026)  [QUEUE ONLY: not registered, not tested, not scored, N +0]
Origin: Joe's own idea, not from a video or paper. Recorded as written, unscored. Needs Joe's sign-off and a reviewer pass before anything is built or run.

The idea: make the agent's risk per trade dynamic. A pre-declared confidence score sets the risk fraction (low confidence about 1% of account, high confidence higher), trades follow predictive statistical signals, and sizing follows fractional Kelly.

Claude Sonnet 5.5 notes (first look, nothing run):
1. Sizing multiplies an edge, it does not create one. With no demonstrated edge today (BFG about -0.01R/trade after realistic fills) larger size on "high confidence" trades only loses faster. Order of work: Family A first, then this only if a signal survives.
2. Fractional Kelly: full Kelly for a bet with win probability p and payoff b (in R) is f = p - (1-p)/b, or mean R / variance of R for a general payoff. Practical use is a fraction (a quarter to a half of full Kelly) because the inputs are estimated with error, and a hard cap. Proposal for the cap: never above 2% of account per trade until there is at least a year of live results. The 10% figure in the original wording is far too large: a 7-loss streak at 10% risk costs about half the account.
3. The testable part, which is cheap: the confidence score must be calibrated, meaning high-score trades really return more R than low-score trades. Design if ever registered: score built only from features fixed in writing beforehand (for example signal strength percentile, volatility regime), logged on paper at flat 1% sizing, then compared across thirds (top versus bottom) after the sample exists.
4. Power (estimate): SD of R about 1.3. With 300 trades, 100 per third, detecting a top-versus-bottom difference at t of 2.8 needs about 2.8 x 1.3 x sqrt(2/100) = 0.51R. That is large, so a 300-trade sample would only confirm a very strong score. More trades or a bigger effect are needed.
5. Related option with published support: volatility-targeted sizing (risk steady when the market gets wild). Verify the paper before relying on it.
6. Cost to register: the score is a new feature set, so it counts as at least +1 N and must be frozen with its kill rule before any look. Not registered.
Status: queued. Next step only if Joe asks.
