## Idea 1 - Opening-range breakout with pullback entry vs its fade (NAS100)
- Source: a YouTube "NASDAQ strategy" video (Nov 2026). The video is also an affiliate advert for a broker and its performance claims are unverifiable; only the mechanical core is used. Discretionary parts (support/resistance, fair value gaps, EMA cross "confirmation", picking "the best level") are NOT testable and are dropped.
- Mechanism claimed: the first 15 minutes of the US cash session set a range; a 5-minute close beyond it signals the day's direction; a pullback entry gives a better price and a tight stop.
- DRAFT rule (to be frozen after review): range = high/low of the 15-minute bar starting 09:30 New York. Signal = first 5-minute bar that CLOSES beyond the range, once per day. Entry (breakout spec): limit at the range edge (retest) or market at the next open if no pullback within 12 bars (choose ONE; reviewer to advise). Stop = opposite side of the range or 1.0 range-width, whichever is nearer (ONE choice). Exit: fixed target 2R, no trailing, no partial, no break-even move, flat at 16:00 New York. Fade spec: the exact mirror (enter against the breakout at the range edge, stop beyond the breakout extreme, target the range midpoint). One breakout spec, one fade spec: N +2.
- Data: existing NAS100 1-minute bars (Nov 2023 to Nov 2026, about 3 years; longer 5-minute history exists for the Nov 2021 to 2026 window but needs the range from the 09:30 bar). Costs: per-bar spread, gap-aware stop fills (bar open beyond the stop fills at that open), no financing (intraday), no commission on index CFDs (to verify).
- Power (estimate): about 750 trading days, perhaps 55-75% trigger a signal, so about 400-550 events per spec; per-trade SD about 1R with a 2R target; detectable mean about 2.64 x 1/sqrt(450) = about 0.12R. So only a large edge is detectable. State this before running.
- Prior it works: about 10% (breakout), about 10% (fade). The video's claims should add nothing to the prior.
- Kill rule: spec fails if validation mean < +0.03R or day-clustered t < 2.0 under realistic fills; then it is closed in writing. No re-tuning of the range length, target or stop after seeing results.
- Counts in N: +2.
- Questions for the reviewer: (1) is the draft rule specific enough to be frozen? (2) is the power too low to be worth running? (3) breakout entry choice (retest limit vs market) and stop choice: which ONE to register? (4) any look-ahead in using the 09:30 bar close? (5) should the long-bias in the index (it drifts up) be controlled by also testing a long-only version, or does that add a look?
